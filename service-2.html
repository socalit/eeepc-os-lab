Android results on the ASUS EeePC 1016P

The Android-x86 builds that were tested, what still worked, and which newer Android desktop forks were simply too heavy for the Atom N455.

Android summary: Android-x86 7.1 R5 was the clear winner. The old Android-x86 4.0 R2 EeePC image had great shortcut support but was too outdated to use. Bliss OS, PrimeOS, and Phoenix OS were too heavy for the CPU and crashed.

Android-x86 4.0 R2 EeePC

Fn shortcuts workToo outdated
  • Shortcut keys and volume controls worked well.
  • Browsers were out of date and app APIs were broken.
  • Apps were no longer practically usable because support had moved on.

Android-x86 7.1 R5

Best optionSpotify worksSome apps crash
  • Trying to upgrade from Android-x86 4.0 R2 EeePC broke the OS.
  • A clean install of 7.1 R5 was required.
  • Smart Dock from F-Droid made the experience much better.
  • Chrome could play YouTube at 240p, but the YouTube app itself was no longer supported.

Working apps on 7.1 R5

Usable set
  • Spotify
  • Discord
  • ChatGPT
  • VLC
  • RetroArch
  • FX File Manager
  • Chrome, with limitations and no current update path

Apps that failed on 7.1 R5

Crashes
  • Updated Chrome crashed
  • Netflix crashed
  • Plex crashed
  • YouTube app unsupported

Bliss OS

Too heavyCrashes
  • Could not be fully tested.
  • Loaded poorly and kept crashing.
  • Main limitation was the Atom N455 CPU.

PrimeOS and Phoenix OS

Too heavyUnstable
  • Both were too much for a 1-core / 2-thread Atom.
  • They were unstable and could not be meaningfully evaluated.
  • The limitation was CPU first, not just the overall age of the laptop.
